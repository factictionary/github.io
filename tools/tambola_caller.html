How to Host a Tambola / Housie Game Using This Online Caller

Tambola (known as Housie in the UK, Australia, and India, and 90-Ball Bingo internationally) uses a master board of numbers from 1 to 90 and player tickets arranged in a 3-row by 9-column grid. Each ticket contains exactly 15 numbers—5 numbers per row—with Column 1 holding numbers 1–9, Column 2 holding 10–19, and so on up to Column 9 (80–90).

  1. Distribute Tickets: Give physical paper tickets to your guests, click 👥 Invite Group on WhatsApp above so every guest gets their own live interactive ticket in their browser, or ask everyone to install All-In-One Tambola & Housie on their phones.
  2. Announce Winning Dividends: Before calling the first number, declare which patterns are active for the round—such as Early Five (Jaldi 5), Four Corners, Top Line, Middle Line, Bottom Line, Breakfast / Lunch / Dinner, and Full House.
  3. Call Numbers with Nicknames: Click Call Next Number (or enable the 5-second Auto-Caller). Our built-in voice announcer reads aloud both the traditional calling nickname (like "Two Little Ducks, 22" or "Top of the House, 90") and highlights the cell on the 1–90 board so you can verify claims in seconds.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the random number generator prevent duplicate calls?

The caller shuffles an array of integers 1 through 90 using the unbiased Fisher-Yates shuffle algorithm in your browser memory, guaranteeing every number is drawn once with uniform mathematical probability.

What happens if a player claims Full House late?

Under standard Tambola rules, a dividend claim is valid only if called before the host draws the next number. If two players claim simultaneously on the exact same number, the prize is split equally. See our Complete Tambola Rules & 1–90 Nicknames Guide for full tournament edge cases.
