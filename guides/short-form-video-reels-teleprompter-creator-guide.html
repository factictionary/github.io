1. The 3-Step Formula for Reading a Mobile Teleprompter Without Eye Movement

Beginners often complain that using a teleprompter makes them "look like a news anchor reading a screen." That only happens when the scrolling text covers the entire width of your phone display. Follow these three physics rules instead:

  1. Anchor Text in the Top 20% Next to the Front Camera: Keep the active reading line within 1.5 cm of the physical front camera cutout. In Teleprompter & AI Speech Coach, position the overlay directly beneath the top camera lens.
  2. Use Narrow 3-to-4 Word Lines: Never let lines stretch across the full screen width. When a line only contains 3 to 4 words, your foveal vision reads the entire line simultaneously with zero horizontal eye darting.
  3. Follow the 70 cm Tripod Distance Rule: The closer your face is to the phone, the more noticeable a 1 cm offset between lens and text becomes. Place your phone on a tripod or stack of books 65–80 cm away (just beyond arm's reach). At that distance, looking 1 cm below the lens is optically indistinguishable from direct eye contact!

2. Scripting for Speaking vs. Scripting for Reading

Written essays sound robotic when spoken aloud. Before pasting your script into the teleprompter, run it through our Word Counter & Reading Time Calculator and apply these edits:

  • Target 140 to 155 Words Per Minute (WPM): A 30-second Short needs exactly 70 to 78 words. A 60-second Reel needs 145 to 155 words.
  • Write Contractions & Visual Breath Marks: Replace "It is important that you do not..." with "Don't...". Insert a double line break // wherever you want to pause for emphasis or change camera gestures.
  • Capitalize Emphasis Words: Capitalize 1 key word per sentence (e.g., "Most creators make ONE fatal mistake in the first two seconds") so your vocal pitch naturally rises and falls instead of sounding monotone.
