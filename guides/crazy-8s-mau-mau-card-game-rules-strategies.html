Crazy Eights (Crazy 8s) & Mau Mau: Complete Rules, Action Cards & Winning Strategy

Master the mathematical probabilities, suit-starvation tactics, and penalty-stacking rules behind the world's most popular shedding card games.

1. What Are Shedding Card Games? (Crazy Eights, Mau Mau & 4-Color Decks)

Crazy Eights (originally popularized in the 1930s as Eights and closely related to the European game Mau Mau, British Switch, and commercial 4-color decks like UNO) belongs to the shedding family of card games. Unlike trick-taking games such as Bridge or Spades—where the goal is to capture high-value cards—your sole objective in a shedding game is to empty your hand before any opponent does.

Despite its simple core mechanic (match either the Color/Suit or the Number/Rank of the top card on the discard pile), high-level Crazy Eights and Mau Mau involve deep probability tracking, hand concealment, and tempo control.

2. Official Setup & Core Rules Comparison

When playing with a standard 52-card deck or a dedicated 4-color action deck, setup takes less than 30 seconds:

  • 2 Players: Deal 7 cards to each player.
  • 3 to 5 Players: Deal 5 cards (standard 52-card deck) or 7 cards (double deck / 108-card 4-color deck) to each player.
  • The Starter Pile: Place the remaining undealt stock face-down in the center and flip the top card face-up beside it to start the discard pile. (If the first flipped card is an 8/Wild, bury it in the middle of the stock and flip a new starter card.)

3. Five Advanced Winning Strategies Used by Top Players

Strategy 1: The "Bridge Card" Hand Architecture

Beginners shed cards randomly within the current suit until they run out. Experienced players organize their hand around Bridge Cards—numbers of which they hold two or more colors/suits (for example, a Red 6 and a Blue 6). Whenever Red is active, shed your singletons (Red cards that have no matching number in another color) first, and save your Red 6 as the final bridge to pivot into Blue on your terms.

Strategy 2: Suit Starvation Tracking

Watch every single time an opponent draws from the stock pile! If the active card on the table is a Green 9 and your opponent draws a card instead of playing, you instantly know two critical facts:

  1. They hold zero Green cards.
  2. They hold zero 9s across all four colors (and likely zero Wild 8s, unless they are sandbagging).

Immediately keep the active color on Green or pivot back to Green as soon as possible to force them to keep drawing.

Strategy 3: Why Hoarding Wild 8s Until the End Can Backfire

While holding an 8 (Wild) as your second-to-last card guarantees an easy out, hoarding three Wilds in a scoring game is dangerous. In point-based Crazy Eights, if another player goes out while you are still holding an 8, each 8 in your hand penalizes you 50 points (compared to 10 points for face cards and face value for Pip cards). If any opponent drops to 2 cards, burn excess high-penalty Wilds immediately to lock them out of their preferred color.

Strategy 4: 1v1 (Two-Player) Tempo Locking

In two-player head-to-head matches (such as 1v1 mode in Duo Cards: Crazy 8s), Reverse cards function identically to Skip cards because reversing direction with two players returns the turn straight back to you. Chain your Reverse, Skip, and Draw-Two cards of the same color together in a single multi-turn combo burst to shed 3 to 4 cards consecutively without giving your opponent a single turn.

Strategy 5: High-Rank Shedding Priority

When you have a choice between playing a Blue 9 or a Blue 3 (and neither is a bridge card), always discard the Blue 9 first. In tournament scoring, getting caught with a 9 costs 9 penalty points, whereas getting caught with a 3 costs only 3 points.

4. Standard Tournament Scoring System

If you play multi-round Crazy Eights with family or friends, the winner of each hand scores points equal to the remaining cards trapped in opponents' hands:

  • Every 8 (or Wild Card): 50 Points
  • King, Queen, Jack (or Skip / Reverse / Draw 2): 10 Points (20 Points in 4-Color rules)
  • Ace: 1 Point
  • Number Cards (2 through 10): Face Value (e.g., a 7 is worth 7 points)

The first player to cross 100 points (2 players), 150 points (3 players), or 200 points (4+ players) wins the match.

5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Can you play a Wild 8 on any turn even if you have a matching color?

Yes! Unlike Wild Draw Four (+4) in commercial rules—which technically requires having no matching color—a standard Wild 8 can be played legally at any time on any turn.

What happens when the draw stock pile runs out of cards?

Keep the top card of the discard pile face-up on the table, take all cards underneath it, shuffle them thoroughly, and place them face-down as the new draw stock.

How many cards do you draw if you cannot play?

In modern fast-paced rules (and in Duo Cards), you draw 1 card from the stock; if it is playable, you may play it immediately, otherwise your turn passes. In classic "Draw Until Playable" house rules, a player must continue drawing until they find a valid card.
